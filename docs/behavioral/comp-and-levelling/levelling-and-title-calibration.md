## In one line

Levelling happens during the interviews, not at the offer, so calibrate early and make sure your stories are told at the scope you're asking to be paid for.

## What it is

Most companies place you in a band before any number is discussed, and the band is set by interviewer feedback about scope. Negotiating comp afterwards moves you within the band; almost nothing moves you between bands once the loop is over.

**Calibrate early.** Ask the recruiter in the first call: what levels is this role open at, what's the band for each, and what distinguishes them here. It's a completely normal question and the answer changes how you prepare. If the role is only open at one level below where you're aiming, that's better known in week one than week five.

**Titles are not portable.** A senior title at a 30-person startup and at a large company describe different jobs, in either direction. What travels is scope: what you owned, how much ambiguity you absorbed, how many people your decisions affected. Describe that and let them map it.

**Interview at the level you want.** Levelling comes from your examples. Stories about implementing well-specified work get scored mid-level however senior the storyteller is. Choose the ones where you scoped, decided, and dealt with the consequences.

**Watch for stretch offers and down-level offers.** A down-level offer with "you can get promoted in six months" is worth asking hard questions about — what's the promotion cycle, who decides, how many people were promoted last cycle. Sometimes it's genuine and the company's bands are simply compressed; often it's the cheapest version of yes.

**Small companies may have no ladder at all.** Then the title is negotiable in a way comp often isn't, and the useful questions are about scope: what am I accountable for, who else is senior, do I own a surface end to end.

## Why it matters

Level determines pay band, scope, and how seriously your opinion is taken from day one, and it compounds — the next role calibrates off this one. It's also the highest-leverage thing to influence, and the influence window closes when the loop does.

## Key points

- Level is set by interviewer feedback on scope, before comp is discussed.
- Ask in the first recruiter call which levels the role is open at and what separates them.
- Titles don't transfer between companies; described scope does.
- Your stories set your level — pick the ones where you decided rather than implemented.
- A promised promotion in six months is a claim; ask about the cycle, the decider, and last cycle's numbers.
- Startups often have no ladder, which makes title negotiable and scope the thing to pin down.
- Down-levelling compounds into the next search, so it deserves a real conversation, not just a yes.
- If levelling looks wrong mid-loop, raise it before the offer — afterwards it's mostly fixed.
